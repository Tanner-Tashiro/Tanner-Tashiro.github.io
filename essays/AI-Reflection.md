## I. Introduction
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become an increasingly used tool in education, especially in technical fields such as Software Engineering. AI systems such as ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, and Google Gemini can help students learn concepts, generate code examples, debug programs, and improve productivity. In software engineering courses like ICS 314, AI tools can function as tutors, pair programmers, documentation assistants, and brainstorming partners.

Throughout ICS 314, I primarily used ChatGPT, and occasionally GitHub Copilot, to support my learning and development process. I used AI for understanding difficult concepts, debugging code, and reviewing assignments. However, I also discovered that AI was not always reliable. Sometimes the generated solutions were incorrect, overly complicated, or failed to follow assignment requirements exactly. As a result, I learned that AI works best as a support tool rather than a replacement for independent problem solving. This essay reflects on my experiences using AI in ICS 314 and analyzes both the benefits and limitations of AI in software engineering education.

## II. Personal Experience with AI
### 1. Experience WODs
I would use ChatGPT during Experience WODs typically only for setting up my environment. Occasionally I would have errors in setting up my environment or would forget maybe a couple of commands to run so having AI helped me with those problems, which was pretty reliable.

### 2. In-class Practice WODs
For practice WODs, I used AI mainly to review concepts after class instead of during the exercises themselves. AI explanations were often easier to follow than technical documentation because they simplified complex topics into step-by-step examples. However, I avoided relying too heavily on AI during practice because I wanted to improve my own problem-solving speed before graded WODs.

### 3. In-class WODs
I avoided using AI during actual in-class WODs because of time pressure and concerns about academic honesty. In situations where I became completely stuck, I occasionally asked short debugging questions such as: "what is this code block missing or needs to resolve this error?"

The responses sometimes pointed out missing return statements or incorrect imports. However, copying code into AI during a timed assignment often took too much time and disrupted my work flow. Overall, I found AI less effective during timed exercises compared to preparation and review.

### 4. Essays
I do not use AI for my essays as I feel it kind of ruins the whole point of it, and its not like the essays are this super complicated or tedious process. Its is simply writing down my thoughts and opinions on certain topics.

### 5. Final Project
During the final project, AI became quite the useful tool. I would often use ChatGPT on debugging or setting up the environment. Early in development, one of the main issues was setting up the Vercel stuff and database; and honestly, I feel that AI helped me tremendously with getting started or setting things up so I can actually get started working. 

AI helped identify bugs, suggest cleaner organization, and explain framework behavior. It was especially useful when searching through documentation became frustrating or time consuming. The biggest benefit was increased productivity, while the biggest risk was blindly accepting incorrect solutions. Also, I was primarily using ChatGPT for M1 but I later starting using CoPilot in M3 which I found to be more useful and probably more reliable since it has direct access to your code so it can run it's own tests.

### 6. Learning a Concept / Tutorial
AI was useful when learning completely new concepts. For example, during the final project, I had difficulty with timezones and syncing that with Vercel. The generated explanation broke the concept into manageable steps and helped me understand the issue more clearly than documentation alone. AI acted almost like a personal tutor because it could adapt explanations and provide additional examples when I asked follow up questions.

### 7. Answering a Question in Class or in Discord
I have not used AI to answer questions in Class or in Discord since that doesn't really make sense to do. Discussions usually are looking for human input, and to gauge your understanding not something that can be searched up online.

### 8. Asking or Answering a Smart Question
AI helped me improve the quality of my questions. Instead of asking vague debugging questions, I used prompts like: “Help me rewrite this programming question with more technical detail.”
This improved my communication skills because I learned how to describe bugs clearly, include relevant context, and ask focused technical questions.

### 9. Coding Example
I didn't really ask for specific coding examples, but sometimes it just automatically gives you them.

### 10. Explaining Code
One of the most useful AI features was explaining confusing code. I frequently pasted small code snippets and asked: “Explain this code line by line.”
This was extremely helpful when reviewing sample code from tutorials or debugging unfamiliar syntax. AI explanations often translated technical code into plain English in a way that improved comprehension. This use of AI felt educational rather than simply productive because it directly supported learning.

### 11. Writing Code
I never really asked AI to write code. Typically I want to create the grounds for AI to work off of and not the other way around. I set my goals and ask AI for assistance and guidance to achieve that goal. 

### 12. Documenting Code
AI was useful for generating comments and documentation. This helped improve readability and encouraged better documentation practices. AI-generated comments were usually accurate for simple functions. However, for more complex logic, AI sometimes misunderstood the purpose of the code and generated misleading documentation.

### 13. Quality Assurance
I feel that this is what I primarily used AI for: debugging and quality assurance. I frequently used prompts like: “What is wrong with this code?”

AI could quickly identify syntax mistakes, formatting issues, and missing imports. This saved significant time during debugging.
Still, AI occasionally suggested fixes that removed warnings while introducing new bugs. This reinforced the importance of testing solutions independently.

### 14. Other Uses in ICS 314 Not Listed
I didn't really use AI for anything else.

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding
AI somewhat influenced my learning experience in ICS 314. The biggest benefit was access to explanations and examples. Instead of waiting for office hours or searching through multiple websites, I could quickly ask questions and receive detailed responses. AI improved my understanding of frameworks such as React by providing simplified explanations and examples. It also increased my confidence when debugging because I could experiment with possible solutions more efficiently.

However, AI also created challenges. Sometimes I relied too heavily on generated solutions instead of struggling through problems independently. I realized that true understanding comes from practicing and debugging independently, not simply copying AI-generated answers.
Overall, AI enhanced my learning when used as a support tool, but it became less helpful when used as a shortcut.

## IV. Practical Applications
Outside ICS 314, AI has become useful in many real-world software engineering contexts. AI-powered coding assistants are increasingly common in industry and help developers improve productivity, automate repetitive tasks, and identify bugs more efficiently. However, real-world use also highlights AI limitations. AI-generated code may contain security vulnerabilities, outdated practices, or inefficient solutions. Developers still need strong foundational knowledge to evaluate whether generated solutions are correct. These experiences showed me that AI is most effective when paired with human judgment and software engineering principles.

## V. Challenges and Opportunities
One major challenge with AI is accuracy. AI sometimes generates incorrect code confidently, which can mislead students who do not fully understand the material. Another challenge is overreliance. Students may become dependent on AI-generated answers instead of developing independent debugging and problem-solving skills.
There are also concerns about academic integrity and fairness. Some students may use AI extensively while others avoid it completely, creating inconsistent learning experiences.

Despite these challenges, AI presents opportunities for software engineering education. AI can provide instant feedback, debugging assistance, and adaptive explanations tailored to different learning styles. This can make technical subjects more accessible and less intimidating for beginners.
The key opportunity is learning how to use AI responsibly and critically rather than passively accepting every answer.

## VI. Comparative Analysis
Traditional teaching methods and AI-enhanced learning each have advantages. Traditional methods such as lectures, readings, and office hours provide structured guidance and encourage deeper thinking through practice and discussion. AI-enhanced learning offers immediacy and personalization. Students can ask unlimited follow-up questions, receive instant explanations, and explore alternative approaches at their own pace.

In terms of engagement, AI can make learning more interactive because students actively explore concepts through conversation. For knowledge retention, however, traditional practice and independent problem-solving may still be more effective because they require deeper cognitive effort. The strongest learning experience likely comes from combining both approaches. Traditional instruction provides foundational understanding, while AI offers supplemental support and personalized assistance.

## VII. Future Considerations
I believe AI will play an larger role in software engineering education in the future. AI systems may eventually provide automated code reviews, personalized assignments, and debugging support integrated directly into development environments. At the same time, educators will need to address concerns about academic honesty, overreliance, and misinformation. Courses may increasingly focus on teaching students how to evaluate AI-generated content critically rather than simply prohibiting AI use.
Future improvements could include AI systems trained specifically on course materials and frameworks so that generated responses align better with class expectations.
Ultimately, the future challenge will not be whether students use AI, but whether they use it responsibly and effectively.

## VIII. Conclusion
My experiences with AI in ICS 314 were both positive and educational. AI tools such as ChatGPT and GitHub Copilot helped me learn concepts faster, debug code more efficiently, and organize assignments. AI acted as a coding assistant and brainstorming partner throughout the course. At the same time, I learned that AI is imperfect and should not replace independent thinking or problem-solving. The most valuable learning occurred when I used AI to support understanding rather than generate complete solutions automatically. Overall, AI has strong potential to improve software engineering education when integrated thoughtfully and responsibly. Future courses should continue encouraging students to use AI critically, ethically, and as a supplement to genuine learning rather than a substitute for it.
